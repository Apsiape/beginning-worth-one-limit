\section{Selection, separated phases and product seeds}\label{sec:selection}
\subsection{What a beginning specifies}
\begin{proposition}[Selection bounds]\label{prop:selection}
If a computable begun orbit converges, its limit is a
$0'$-computable stationary state, TI for a TI seed. In general
both ordinary and Ces\`aro cluster classes have $\Pi^0_2$
valid-code descriptions; only the latter is automatically
stationary. At computable $K$, a depth-$d$ law at a state named by $X$ is computable
from $X^{(d)}$. For $d\geq1$, a selected value outside
$\D_{d+1}$ forces the state to have no low name.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each limiting cylinder coordinate of a convergent computable
orbit is a uniformly computable-sequence limit, hence uniformly
$0'$-computable. Weak continuity gives stationarity. The cluster
formula \eqref{eq:cluster-pi2} applies to any computable measure
sequence, including ordinary orbits. Equation~\eqref{eq:cesaro-residual}
supplies the additional stationarity for Ces\`aro clusters.
The relative law bound is the iterated limit argument of
Section~\ref{sec:consensus}. At $d\geq1$, a low name satisfies
$X^{(d)}\equiv_T0^{(d)}$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

There is no unrestricted one-jump point-selection conclusion.
A $\Pi^0_2$ description of a nonempty compact cluster class
does not supply a $0'$-computable member. Computable bounded
sequences can lack any limit-computable accumulation point
\cite[Corollary~11.8]{bgm}. This does not by itself exhibit a
positive-rate PCA with that property; it invalidates the inference
from description complexity alone.

The restriction $d\geq1$ in the non-lowness statement matters.
Take a noncomputable low set $A$ and its base-four Cantor real
$r$, of the same degree. In the computable identity CA,
$(1-r)\delta_{0^\Z}+r\delta_{1^\Z}$ is stationary, has a low name,
and its depth-zero local expectation is the noncomputable $r$.
No effective class pins that expectation. This example concerns
the general computable-PCA selection statement; the identity
rule is not a positive-rate realization.

Ordinary accumulation need not mean stationarity, even with
positive rates. Let $T$ be symmetric low-noise binary Toom
dynamics and $C$ global complement. They commute, and
$T^t\delta_0\to\mu_-$, $T^t\delta_1\to\mu_+$ with
$\mu_-\ne\mu_+$. For $P=CT$, the even and odd subsequences
from $\delta_0$ tend to $\mu_-$ and $\mu_+$, which $P$
interchanges. Neither is $P$-stationary. The Ces\`aro orbit
converges to their stationary equal mixture.

\subsection{Sharpness and unrestricted mixtures}
The Toom phases in this example follow from the classical
stability theorem \cite{toom,gacstoom}. At computable symmetric
noise $p<1/2$, the kernel is attractive. Starting from the two
constant configurations gives monotone measure sequences and
stationary limits $\mu_+,\mu_-$ with local one probabilities
$m>1/2$ and $1-m$. Stability supplies a uniform separation.
Their increasing-event probabilities are monotone limits of
computable values; arbitrary cylinder coordinates follow by
finite inclusion-exclusion. Both phase measures are uniformly
$0'$-computable.

For any real $r\in[0,1]$ their mixture has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture}
 \mu_r=r\mu_++(1-r)\mu_-,\qquad
 \mu_r(x_0=1)=1-m+r(2m-1).
\end{equation}
Free selection therefore gives a nondegenerate interval of
depth-zero eternal values, including nonarithmetical ones.
No arithmetic ceiling applies to arbitrary selected states.
No robust growing-majority formula for recovering $r$ is needed.

This also gives the depth-zero specification boundary. If $m$
is noncomputable choose $r=0$. Otherwise choose a noncomputable
$r\in\D_2\cap[0,1]$; the local expectation in \eqref{eq:mixture}
then computes $r$ and is noncomputable. In both cases $\mu_r$
is a limit of computable genuine measures: approximate the
weight rationally and use finite Toom evolutions from the
constant seeds. Its singleton consequently has the $\Pi^0_2$
cluster description, but cannot be a nonempty effective
$\Sigma^0_2$ consensus class for that depth-zero statistic.
Theorem~\ref{thm:clockfree} supplies the robust depth-one
boundary with an entire stationary Ces\`aro cluster class.

\subsection{Separated stationary phases in the realization}
\begin{proposition}[Scoped divergence in one automaton]
\label{prop:divergence}
For every $K\in U_\beta$ in the realization there are separated
extreme stationary TI states. At computable $K$, a nonempty
effective $\Sigma^0_2$ stationary class cannot have any
depth-one consensus outside $\D_2$, whereas the canonical begun
law is $\beta$. The statement concerns the same dynamics.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The finite diagonal symbol map of $F$ has a periodic orbit.
Start the noisy stack from its spatially constant cloned row.
Let $E$ mean that original offsets zero and one in a block agree;
$B=2r\geq2$ makes this a one-block event. Full-symbol reliability
gives $\Pr(E)\geq19/20$ at all times. Every Ces\`aro cluster
state $\mu_c$ therefore has $\mu_c(E)\geq19/20$.

From the canonical cloned Bernoulli seed, the noiseless pair-disagreement
probability tends to $2/(1+\mathbb EG)\geq1/3$, since every one
in the renewal measure is flanked by zeros. Reliability gives
$\mu_b(E)\leq2/3+1/20=43/60$ in every begun Ces\`aro cluster
state. Both classes are stationary TI by Ces\`aro averaging.
Maximize and minimize the continuous affine functional $\mu(E)$
over the compact convex stationary class. The corresponding
extremizing faces contain extreme points of the full class,
so separated extreme phases exist.

At computable $K$ the nonempty region
$R=\{\mu\in\ST(K):\mu(E)\leq3/4\}$ is effectively closed.
It contains all the begun Ces\`aro cluster states and excludes
the constant-trajectory class. The consensus exclusion follows
from Theorem~\ref{thm:ceilings}; the begun value follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:realization}. When $\beta\notin\D_2$ the
clock-free selected cluster class is a strictly more restrictive
specification than any such effective closed consensus class.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two limited product-seed obstructions}
\begin{proposition}[Finite-time cones and invariant decoders]
\label{prop:product}
From an i.i.d.\ seed, time-$t$ restrictions to sets separated by
more than $2rt$ are independent for a radius-$r$ PCA. At fixed
finite $t$, a measurable symbol decoder invariant under both
transverse shifts is almost surely constant. A countable row of
such decoded symbols is deterministic; it is homogeneous if the
decoder is also equivariant under longitudinal shifts.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Restrictions are functions of the initial symbols and update
coins in their backward cones. At the stated separation these
input sets are disjoint. A fixed-time plane field is a finite-range
factor of the i.i.d.\ input slab, hence mixing and ergodic under
transverse shifts. An invariant finite-valued function is
therefore almost surely constant. Countable intersection gives
a deterministic row. Longitudinal equivariance and TI force its
coordinates to agree. Without that equivariance a decoder could
output a predetermined alternating row.
\end{proof}

Independent odd majorities need not converge to a deterministic
word: a reset PCA outputting fair bits has fair majority at every
copy count. Strict full-symbol majority also differs from
plurality when, for example, probabilities are $(.4,.35,.25)$.
We do not use a universal modal-word conclusion.

\begin{proposition}[Direct comparison to a cloned reference]
\label{prop:basin}
An i.i.d.\ seed with marginal $q$ can disagree with a cloned row
on a set of plane density less than one half in every plane
only when that row is homogeneous.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Simultaneous strong laws for the finitely many symbols and
countably many planes give disagreement density $1-q(y(i))$.
Thus $q(y(i))>1/2$ in every plane. At most one symbol can satisfy
this inequality, so $y$ is constant, even if selected after
observing the configuration. A deterministic finite-alphabet
line rule takes homogeneous rows through an eventually periodic
computable symbol orbit.
\end{proof}
This excludes only direct initialization of a nonhomogeneous
cloned reference by treating the raw product seed as its sparse
perturbation. It excludes neither preprocessing, changing local
references, hierarchical encoding nor self-organizing correction.
Density less than one half is not the full hypothesis of every
fault theorem. No universal impossibility or clock-necessity
claim follows.
