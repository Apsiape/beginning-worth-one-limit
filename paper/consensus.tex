\section{Effective consensus and arithmetic ceilings}\label{sec:consensus}
\subsection{Finite probabilities and compact stationary classes}
For any computable initial measure, the probability of a fixed
time-$t$ cylinder is uniformly computable. Enumerate assignments
on its finite backward cone and sum the initial cylinder weights
times products of the corresponding transition probabilities.
The number of terms is immaterial to computability. The same formula
shows that $\mu\mapsto\mu P_K$ is computable and weakly continuous.

\begin{lemma}[Effective stationary classes]\label{lem:stationary}
For computable $K$, the valid Cantor codes of $\ST(K)$ form a
nonempty $\Pi^0_1$ class. Adding uniformly computable non-strict
cylinder inequalities keeps the class effectively closed.
Every nonempty such class has a low member.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a cylinder indicator $f$, stationarity is the equation
$\mu(f)=\mu(P_Kf)$. The latter function is a finite computable
linear combination of cylinder indicators. Normalization,
marginal consistency, TI equations and stationarity are a
computable list of equalities of computable continuous coordinate
functions. Failure has a finite positive-precision witness.
The same holds for failure of a non-strict inequality.

Take any TI seed and let
$C_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t<T}\lambda P_K^t$. Compactness supplies cluster
states, TI passes to limits, and for $|f|\leq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cesaro-residual}
 |C_T(P_Kf)-C_T(f)|\leq 2/T.
\end{equation}
Every such cluster state is stationary, proving nonemptiness.

Apply the low basis theorem of Jockusch--Soare \cite{lowbasis}.
Here is the particular compact argument. Starting with a nonempty
effective closed code class $P_e$, let $V_e$ be the effective open
set of oracles on which machine $e$ halts on input $e$. Emptiness
of $P_e\setminus V_e$ is semidecidable by finite-cover compactness,
and hence decidable by $0'$. Retain that difference if nonempty;
otherwise retain $P_e$. The previous finite choices can be
hard-coded into an ordinary index for the next closed class.
The nested compact intersection is nonempty. Every code in it
has exactly the $0'$-computable jump answers just recorded.
Its jump is therefore equivalent to $0'$. This is an existence
argument for a low state, not a computable point selector.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Consensus, including the zero-depth case}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ceilings}]
On a low name $X$ in an effective closed class, all finite statistics
are uniformly $X$-computable. Replace each real term of a converging
sequence by a rational within $2^{-s}$. With $X'$, decide whether a
candidate rational Cauchy-tail bound fails: failure is an existential
statement about rational terms. Search for a tail satisfying a
slightly stronger bound than the requested tolerance. Its limit is
$X'$-computable. Relativize uniformly in all remaining indices and
iterate. At depth $d\geq1$ the value is computable from
$X^{(d)}\equiv_T0^{(d)}$, hence belongs to $\D_{d+1}$.

At $d=0$, lowness alone is insufficient. Instead let $P$ be the
nonempty effective compact code class and $s$ the computable
continuous statistic, defined on it and constant with value $v$.
Enumerate rational open intervals $I$ of width at most $2^{-n}$.
The condition $P\subset s^{-1}(I)$ is semidecidable: the effective
open complement of $P$ together with the effective open preimage
must cover Cantor space, and a finite basic cover witnesses this.
An interval containing $v$ in its interior eventually succeeds.
Its midpoint computes $v$ to the required precision. Partial
definedness causes no difficulty because the compact class is
contained in the statistic's open domain.

For a nonempty effective $\Sigma^0_2$ code class, choose a nonempty
closed piece. The common value on that piece has the preceding
bound. The choice is a finite nonuniform constant; no algorithm
selecting the piece is claimed. Pieces need not be saturated under
alternative names, since their decoded images remain effectively
compact and the value agrees on all their codes.

For the pure-state assertion, $\ST(K)$ is metrizable compact
convex. Choquet representation \cite{choquet} expresses each
member as a barycentre of a probability measure on its extreme
boundary. For bounded linear statistics,
$\mu(F_{\mathbf k})=\int e(F_{\mathbf k})\,dw_\mu(e)$.
At each fixed outer tuple the innermost pointwise limit on extreme
states is bounded Borel. Dominated convergence moves that limit
through the integral. Repeat through every remaining limit.
All intermediate mixture limits exist, and agreement at the
extremes forces agreement everywhere. No effective decomposition
or effective presentation of the extreme boundary is required.

At computable $\lambda,K$, finite-time statistics in
\eqref{eq:begun} are uniformly computable. One time limit and $d$
scale limits give a value computable in $0^{(d+1)}$. For a robust
value, evaluate at a rational kernel in the nonempty open region.
Equality transfers that absolute ceiling to all kernels there;
there is no uniform procedure locating this witness from an
arbitrary presentation of the open set.

Finally, finite stationary-history cylinders are computable from
the state name and $K$ by finite-cone sums. Thus the same argument
works for these readouts. Each limiting lag is an index among the
$d$ limits, not a free observation-time operation.
\end{proof}

The pure-state extension is specifically linear and bounded.
The nonlinear map $p\mapsto p(1-p)$, where $p=\mu(x_0=1)$,
vanishes on the two constant-state phases of the noiseless AND
automaton but equals $1/4$ at their equal mixture. This already
shows why the Choquet step cannot be applied to arbitrary ratios.
To check the example, TI stationarity for AND gives
$\mu(1)=\mu(11)$, hence $\mu(10)=\mu(01)=0$, so the stationary TI
class consists exactly of those constant-state mixtures.

Computability of a unique invariant measure for a computable PCA
was proved by Marcovici--Sablik--Taati
\cite[Proposition~2.2]{marcovici}. The compact-consensus mechanism
also belongs to the effective invariant-measure framework of
Galatolo--Hoyrup--Rojas \cite{ghr}. Our comparison uses these
classical principles for nested laws, rather than claiming a new
computability mechanism for unique stationary measures.
